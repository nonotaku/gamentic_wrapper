## Verdict
**7.5/10 — feature it as a curated emotional highlight, not a retention driver.** *Dear Mom* is a short (few-minute), free, browser-native puzzle-platformer with a genuine emotional payload: players report crying and wanting to hug their mothers. It's polished (built in Construct, interactive tutorial, no generative AI), universally relatable, and clean of ads or monetization traps — ideal for a "play in one sitting" showcase. The caveats are length and thin data: a perfect 5.0 rests on only 11 ratings, and there's little replay value. As a signature short-form piece it shines; as a daily engagement anchor it won't hold. Worth featuring, positioned honestly as a brief, moving experience.

## Gameplay
A narrative puzzle-platformer framed as a bittersweet homecoming. The core loop is small environmental puzzles woven into a story about a mother — e.g. kicking a ball onto valves, or a photography mechanic where you resize objects (maximizing "the size of the stairs") to progress. The genre works when mechanics *carry* the emotion rather than decorate it: each solved puzzle advances a memory, so problem-solving and storytelling reinforce each other. Difficulty is gentle by design; the reward is emotional, not mechanical mastery.

## Why it ranks
The itch "#1 popularity" metric is a live, momentum-based score easily topped by a small viral spike, so read it as "currently trending in its niche," not "best puzzle game." It earns the traction legitimately, though: free + browser-instant + a few minutes long = near-zero friction, and an emotional "made me cry" hook is exactly what gets shared and screen-capped. A 5.0/11 rating signals strong sentiment from a self-selected audience, not broad validation — high ceiling, small sample.

## Design notes
Expect keyboard + mouse controls, a guided tutorial, and tight one-sitting pacing — good UX hygiene for a portal. Being an itch HTML5 build, there are **no injected ads or dark patterns**; monetization is absent (free, with optional donation at most). Watch-outs are curatorial, not ethical: the run is very short and essentially linear, so replay and session time are low; the "sad mom" framing is emotionally deliberate — powerful, but tonally heavy, so flag it for players expecting a light puzzler. Verify browser fit and mobile/touch behavior before featuring, since Construct exports are keyboard-oriented.
