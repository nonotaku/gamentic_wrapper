## Ranking
1. **2048** — 10/10 — A flawless distillation of "one rule, infinite depth"; the benchmark every browser puzzle is measured against.
2. **A Dark Room** — 9/10 — Minimalist text that mutates into something haunting; the rare idle game with authorial intent.
3. **Hextris** — 8/10 — Tetris rethought on a spinning hexagon; fast, tense, and genuinely original.
4. **Elevator Saga** — 8/10 — You write real JavaScript to solve it; a brilliant coding puzzle, if you're the audience.
5. **Cube Composer** — 7/10 — Functional programming taught as a puzzle; clever, calm, quietly educational.
6. **HexGL** — 7/10 — A gorgeous three.js anti-grav racer; the visuals outrun the thin gameplay loop.
7. **T-Rex Runner** — 6/10 — The iconic Chrome dino, faithfully extracted — but it's a port, not a design.
8. **Astray** — 5/10 — A WebGL+Box2D maze that impresses as tech and forgets to be fun.
9. **Clumsy Bird** — 5/10 — A polished, honest Flappy Bird clone; competent, derivative, forgettable.
10. **Pacman (mumuy)** — 4/10 — A clean HTML5 Pac-Man built as a dev sample; a tutorial, not a game.

## Top pick
**2048** wins on every axis this group is judged by. Its 13,341 stars aren't nostalgia — they're the residue of near-perfect design: four inputs, one merge rule, and an emergent skill ceiling that keeps pulling players back. It loads instantly, plays on any device, and needs zero explanation. In a lineup that includes tech demos and clones, 2048 is the only entry that is *simultaneously* original, endlessly replayable, and technically effortless. It's the platonic ideal of a portal game.

## Notable mentions
- **A Dark Room** — The group's boldest creative swing. It weaponizes minimalism, letting mechanics and meaning unfold through restraint. Nothing else here has narrative ambition, and its 8,193 stars prove players reward surprise.
- **Elevator Saga** — Turns the puzzle itself into a programming environment. It's niche, but as a design idea — the player's solution *is* code — it's the most conceptually daring thing in the list.
- **Hextris** — The best-executed "original arcade" entry: the hexagon rotation reframes a familiar stacking loop into something that feels new and reads instantly. Polish and clarity working together.

## Skip these
- **Pacman (mumuy)** — Explicitly a development sample (经典游戏开发样例). It's reference code, not a designed experience, and adds nothing over the arcade original.
- **Astray** — A procedurally-generated 3D maze that's really a WebGL/Box2D showcase. Rolling a ball to an exit is thin; the 598 stars reflect how little play is underneath the tech. Admire the engine, skip the game.
- **Clumsy Bird** (conditional) — Fine if you want Flappy Bird, but it's a straight clone. Worth keeping only as a MelonJS demo, not as a distinct pick.

## Group insights
This is a snapshot of the open-source/GitHub browser-game canon, and it splits cleanly into two tribes. One tribe — 2048, A Dark Room, Hextris, Elevator Saga, Cube Composer — leads with a *novel mechanic* and treats the browser as a constraint. The other — HexGL, Astray, Pacman, Clumsy Bird, arguably T-Rex — leads with *technology or homage*, using a familiar loop to show off an engine or teach a codebase.

The star counts tell the lesson unambiguously: the mechanic-first tribe dominates the top, and clones/tech-demos cluster at the bottom regardless of visual flash. HexGL is the sharpest warning — the prettiest game here ranks mid-pack because spectacle doesn't survive the second play.

For a developer: **an original rule beats a beautiful renderer.** Scope small, make the core interaction surprising, and make it load instantly. 2048 shipped a single idea perfectly and outscored every technically fancier neighbor combined.
