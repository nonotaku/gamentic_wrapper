## Verdict
**9/10 — feature it.** Sort the Court is a genuine indie classic: a free, browser-ready (HTML5) binary-choice kingdom sim built by Graeme Borland for Ludum Dare 34 (2015), now sitting at 4.7★ from ~10,900 ratings. It is charming, instantly understandable, one-input accessible, and — critically for a portal — carries **no ads, no microtransactions, and no dark patterns** in the itch build (donations are explicitly optional). Its only real limit is a finite event pool, so deep replayability tapers once you've seen most petitioners. For a "pick up, smile, share" featured slot, it's close to ideal.

## Gameplay
You are a monarch holding court. Petitioners approach one at a time with a request, and you answer **Yes or No**. Each choice nudges three resources — population, gold, and happiness — and gold + citizens let you buy upgrades (fountain, walls, buildings) that grow the city toward a population goal. The loop is: *read request → guess the consequence → watch stats shift → reinvest → unlock the next thing.* The genre ticks because of low friction and high curiosity: binary choices remove analysis paralysis, while writing variety, light humor, and unpredictable outcomes ("what happens if I say yes to the dragon?") supply the pull. It's a resource-juggle wrapped in a joke-a-turn narrative.

## Why it ranks
Several forces compound: it's **free and runs in-browser**, so the barrier to a 5-star click is near zero; the art (by amymja) is disarmingly cute and the soundtrack (Bogdan Rybak) is memorable; the one-button design travels to keyboard, mouse, gamepad, and mobile (Poki). Add Ludum Dare jam pedigree, wholesome tone, and years of viral portal exposure, and you get the enormous 10k+ rating count that stabilizes the score at 4.7. High sample size + universally approachable design = a durable, trustworthy rating.

## Design notes
Expect: single Yes/No input, a three-stat economy, building/upgrade purchases, event variety, and light quest chains. UX is clean and readable — good for a portal audience. **Watch out for one thing:** the *Poki mobile* version is ad-served by that platform; the itch **HTML5 build is clean**, so self-host that build rather than embedding Poki's. Also flag expectations: sessions are "a few minutes" and content is finite, so market it as a delightful short experience, not an endless grinder.
